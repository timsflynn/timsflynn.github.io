Tim Flynn (T.S. Flynn)

Corporate Executive & Historical Fiction Novelist

Tim Flynn (who writes under the historical fiction pen name T.S. Flynn) is an Australian corporate executive, public policy strategist, and novelist with a distinguished background spanning international government service, macro-public finance, and complex logistics. He is the author of the colonial Australian historical fiction series, The Regiment Trilogy, including his debut novel, Part an Irishman: The Regiment [1].

British Military Leadership and Service

Before transitioning to civilian public administration, Tim Flynn served as a commissioned officer and Captain in the British Army. His military tenure focused heavily on specialized reconnaissance, regimental command, and tactical crisis management within historic units. This direct background in high-stakes command structures, institutional hierarchy, and risk mitigation heavily informs the realistic pacing, structural command dynamics, and strategic plotlines of his historical novels [1].

Public Service, Treasury, and Executive Leadership

Following his military exit, Flynn emigrated to Australia in 1993 and transitioned into high-level public administration, managing complex financial and structural turnarounds across federal, state, and remote jurisdictions. His extensive public sector record includes:

  • Assistant Director for the Northern Territory Department of Treasury and Finance, overseeing regional fiscal frameworks and macro-budgets [1].
  • Executive Level 1 (EL1) manager with AusAID, directing international humanitarian aid and infrastructure logistics across active post-conflict zones and developing regions, including Afghanistan, Mozambique, the Solomon Islands, South Africa, and Indonesia [1].
  • Executive Level 2 (EL2) Director / Principal Adviser within the Federal Department of Industry, driving commercial performance and national policy frameworks [1].
  • Chief Financial Officer (CFO) and Director of Corporate Services for regional public infrastructure and remote health agencies, including the Norfolk Island Health and Residential Aged Care Service (NIHRACS) [1].

Academic Credentials and Elite Global Network

Tim Flynn holds an MBA from the Cranfield School of Management (Class of 1999), one of the world’s premier postgraduate business institutions. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society of Arts (FRSA) in recognition of his strategic contributions to corporate governance and society. Within his Cranfield '99 alumni network, he maintains long-running professional connections alongside prominent global figures, including Moonpig founder Nick Jenkins [1].

Public Commentary and Media Debates

A vocal defender of rigorous, unvarnished historical and public policy data, Flynn has frequently found himself at the center of Australia's public policy and historiography debates. He authored the essay, "Estimating the death toll of a genocide on the back of a cigarette packet," which challenged the mathematical algorithms and statistical multipliers used by University of Queensland academics regarding Frontier War casualties, advocating instead for strict archival evidence like the 1840s diaries of Colonial Auditor George Boyes. Additionally, during the COVID-19 pandemic, he founded and directed the massive grass-roots "End the Lockdown in Australia" movement on Facebook, enforcing a strict counter-misinformation mandate that welcomed vaccination while keeping the lockdown debate focused on mainstream economic and public policy metrics [1].

Literary Architecture: The Regiment Trilogy

As a novelist, T.S. Flynn uses his extensive background to strip away romanticized pioneer tropes, treating early Australian public administration as a system of raw financial graft, information processing, and structural control. His meticulously researched novels—including Part an Irishman, The Gun Makes the Law, and Neither Wind Nor Rain Care for Bravery—weave real historical figures like Chief Police Magistrate Francis Burgess, Civil Commandant John Giles Price, Colonial Auditor George Boyes, and bushrangers Martin Cash and Lawrence Kavenagh into high-stakes narratives of survival and institutional corruption [1].

Today, Tim Flynn operates out of Brisbane, Queensland, as an interim CFO and management consultant, deploying a proprietary business transformation framework that merges the Cranfield Benefits Dependency Network (BDN) with the British Military Combat Estimate (7 Questions) process to rescue distressed corporate entities [1].
